Overall Function of the Respiratory System

The respiratory system helps the body breathe. Its main job is to bring oxygen into the body and remove carbon dioxide, which is a waste gas.

Oxygen is important because body cells need it to make energy. Without oxygen, cells cannot work properly. The respiratory system works closely with the circulatory system because the blood carries oxygen from the lungs to the rest of the body.

How Breathing Works

Breathing happens because of a muscle called the diaphragm. When you inhale, the diaphragm moves down and the chest cavity gets larger. This pulls air into the lungs.

When you exhale, the diaphragm moves up and the chest cavity gets smaller. This pushes air out of the lungs.

Gas Exchange in the Alveoli

The most important part of the respiratory system is the alveoli. Alveoli are tiny air sacs inside the lungs. They have very thin walls and are surrounded by tiny blood vessels called capillaries.

Alveoli
⇄
Capillaries / Blood

Oxygen moves from the alveoli into the blood. At the same time, carbon dioxide moves from the blood into the alveoli so it can be breathed out. This process is called gas exchange.

This shows how structure and function are connected: alveoli are tiny, thin, and surrounded by blood vessels, so gases can move quickly and easily.
