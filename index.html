The Journey of Oxygen Through the Respiratory System

The Journey of Oxygen

Imagine one tiny oxygen molecule starting a trip through your body. It does not just magically appear in your blood — it has to travel through several important parts of the respiratory system first.

Click each bubble to follow the path: nose → throat → windpipe → lungs → blood.

Big idea: oxygen travels step by step. The most important stop is the alveoli, because that is where oxygen finally enters the blood.

Overall Function of the Respiratory System

The respiratory system helps the body breathe. Its main job is to bring oxygen into the body and remove carbon dioxide, which is a waste gas.

Oxygen matters because body cells need it to make energy. Without enough oxygen, cells cannot work properly. The respiratory system also works closely with the circulatory system, because blood carries oxygen from the lungs to the rest of the body.

Simple version: breathing brings in the good gas, oxygen, and gets rid of the waste gas, carbon dioxide.

How Breathing Works

Breathing is not just “air going in and out.” It happens because of a muscle called the diaphragm.

When you inhale, the diaphragm moves down, the chest cavity gets bigger, and air is pulled into the lungs.

When you exhale, the diaphragm moves up, the chest cavity gets smaller, and air is pushed out.

Easy way to remember: diaphragm down = air in. Diaphragm up = air out.

Gas Exchange in the Alveoli

The most important part of the respiratory system is the alveoli. Alveoli are tiny air sacs inside the lungs. They have thin walls and are surrounded by tiny blood vessels called capillaries.

Capillaries / Blood

Oxygen moves from the alveoli into the blood. At the same time, carbon dioxide moves from the blood into the alveoli so it can be breathed out.

Why alveoli are useful: they are tiny, thin, and surrounded by blood vessels, so gases can move quickly.

Vocabulary Words

Click each card to see the definition. These are the main respiratory system terms used in this project.

Respiratory System
The body system that brings in oxygen and removes carbon dioxide.
Oxygen
A gas the body needs to make energy.
Carbon Dioxide
A waste gas that the body needs to remove.
Nose
An opening that filters, warms, and moistens air.
Mouth
Another opening where air can enter the body.
Nasal Cavity
The space inside the nose where air is warmed and filtered.
Pharynx
The throat; a passageway for air.
Larynx
The voice box; air passes through it before entering the trachea.
Trachea
The windpipe that carries air toward the lungs.
Bronchi
Two large tubes that carry air from the trachea into the lungs.
Bronchioles
Small air tubes inside the lungs.
Alveoli
Tiny air sacs where gas exchange happens.
Lungs
The main organs of breathing.
Diaphragm
A muscle under the lungs that helps you breathe.
Inhalation
Breathing in air.
Exhalation
Breathing out air.
Gas Exchange
The movement of oxygen into the blood and carbon dioxide out of the blood.
Capillaries
Tiny blood vessels that surround the alveoli.
Hemoglobin
A protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen.
Cilia
Tiny hair-like structures that move mucus and dust out of the airway.
Mucus
Sticky fluid that traps dust, germs, and dirt.
